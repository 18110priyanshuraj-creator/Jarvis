# JARVIS Creator Studio Playbook
## Canva editing, stick-figure explainers, long-form YouTube, and channel improvement

**Purpose:** Give JARVIS a practical, honest production workflow for educational and general-audience videos. This is a creator guide, not a promise of views or a substitute for reviewing the finished edit.

## 1. Start with the viewer, not the editor

Before opening Canva, write a one-sentence creative brief:

- **Audience:** one clearly described group of viewers.
- **Problem/question:** what they want to understand or solve.
- **Promise:** the specific useful result this video will deliver.
- **Proof:** what examples, demonstration, source, or visual will make the promise credible.
- **Format:** 16:9 long-form explainer, 9:16 vertical Short, or square post.
- **Language and tone:** choose the language the audience naturally understands.

Reject topics that are only broad labels (for example, “science”). Narrow them into a clear question (for example, “Why does a metal spoon feel colder than a wooden spoon in the same room?”). Check factual claims against reliable sources and keep a source list for the description.

## 2. Canva video workflow

Canva's current video editor uses a multi-track timeline for new video designs. On mobile, a template-first flow may be available depending on the Canva app/version and account rollout.

1. Open Canva and choose **Video**. For a normal YouTube video, choose a landscape 16:9 canvas; for a Short, choose vertical 9:16.
2. Start blank when the story needs custom pacing, or choose a template only as a layout starting point. Replace its media, colours, type and wording; do not leave template filler text.
3. Upload recorded footage, voiceover, screenshots and original graphics using **Uploads**. Use only media you own, have permission to use, or are licensed to use.
4. Put the primary video clips on the main/bottom track. Place captions, titles, diagrams, images and supporting footage on upper tracks.
5. Trim by dragging a clip's edges. Move the playhead to inspect cuts. Split a clip at the required point when a new segment or transition is needed.
6. Add a transition between clips on the main track only when it helps communicate a change. Prefer clean cuts for explanations; avoid an effect on every cut.
7. Add voiceover/audio on its own track. Keep speech clear above music; reduce or remove background music under important dialogue. Use music and sound effects only when the licence permits the intended use.
8. Add readable captions. Check names, technical words, Hindi/English transliteration and punctuation manually; automatic captions can be wrong.
9. Preview the full timeline from beginning to end, checking timing, spelling, audio, safe margins, aspect ratio and whether each visual matches the narration.
10. Export MP4 at the highest sensible quality available for the account. Watch the exported file once on a phone before upload; do not assume the editor preview is identical to the final file.

**Mobile tip:** If you start from a template, Canva may offer a template-first workflow where you select media, preview, then open the full editor for more detailed work. Feature availability can vary by app version and rollout.

## 3. Build a simple stick-figure character

A stick-figure explainer works when the character is readable and consistent, not when it has hundreds of details.

### Make the base character
1. Create a head from one circle.
2. Add a straight or slightly curved line for the torso.
3. Add two arms and two legs with simple line elements.
4. Add small circles at shoulders, elbows, hips and knees if they help the pose read clearly.
5. Keep line width, head size, colours and proportions consistent.
6. Group the parts when they should move together. Keep limbs separate when you need a different pose.

### Create motion in Canva
- Make one clean **neutral pose** first. Duplicate it to create new poses.
- For each duplicate, change only a few limb angles or the character's position. Small changes make motion easier to follow.
- Use the **Animate** controls on an element or page for simple entrances, exits or motion effects. Preview speed and timing.
- To imitate frame-by-frame motion, create a sequence of duplicated pages/scenes with slightly different poses and short durations. This is a simple visual trick, not a full skeletal-rig or professional frame-by-frame animation system.
- Use camera framing and on-screen arrows, labels or sound cues to make the action obvious.
- Reuse the same character model across the whole video. Do not change its head/limb proportions between scenes.

### Basic pose library
Prepare: idle/standing, walking, pointing, thinking, surprised, explaining, sitting, and celebration. Each pose should communicate one idea. For a long explanation, change pose when the idea changes—not every second.

### Storyboard columns
For each scene write: **Narration | Character pose/action | Background/prop | On-screen words | Sound cue | Duration**. Keep on-screen wording shorter than the voiceover.

## 4. Long-form YouTube structure

There is no magic duration that guarantees distribution. Let the topic earn its length. A long video should feel complete, not stretched.

### Practical structure
- **0:00–0:15 — Hook:** show the surprising result, question, problem or promise. Skip a long logo animation and generic “welcome back” intro.
- **0:15–0:35 — Contract:** tell viewers what they will understand by the end and why it matters.
- **Main sections — Explain in steps:** break the topic into 3–6 meaningful sections. Each section answers one question and leads naturally to the next.
- **Proof/demo:** show the experiment, example, calculation, screen recording or evidence. Do not rely only on claims.
- **Pattern changes:** switch between the stick figure, close-up, diagram, text, example, and recap when the idea changes. These should clarify the story, not distract.
- **Payoff:** resolve the opening question and deliver the promised result.
- **Ending:** one relevant next step or next video suggestion. Do not add a long unrelated outro.

### Hook patterns (write original wording)
- **Question:** “Why does [specific thing] happen even when [surprising condition]?”
- **Contrast:** “Most people expect [A], but the evidence shows [B].”
- **Demonstration:** show a result first, then ask how it happened.
- **Mistake/fix:** “This common mistake changes [outcome]. Here is how to check it.”
- **Story:** start at the moment a real problem appears, then explain the chain of events.

The opening must pay off the title/thumbnail promise quickly. Never make a sensational promise that the actual video cannot support.

## 5. Title and thumbnail

- One clear subject, one idea, one visual focal point.
- Thumbnail: use a readable subject/character, strong contrast, uncluttered background and very few words. Test legibility at small mobile size.
- Title: concise, accurate, specific; put the key idea early. Use either a searchable description or a curiosity-led angle, but never misrepresent the content.
- Make the thumbnail and title complementary instead of repeating the exact same sentence.
- Do not use fake shock, unrelated famous faces, misleading before/after claims, or fabricated statistics.

## 6. Improve reach by improving viewer satisfaction

**No tool or recipe can guarantee 100% reach, viral views, or a particular number of subscribers.** YouTube recommendations are personalized. YouTube describes performance using appeal (whether people choose to watch), engagement (whether they keep watching), and satisfaction (whether they enjoyed the video).

Review the analytics after publication:
- **Impressions and click-through rate:** compare similar videos and traffic sources; do not judge CTR without knowing where impressions came from.
- **First 30 seconds / audience retention:** look for a sharp early drop. If viewers leave, check whether the opening was slow or did not match the title/thumbnail.
- **Retention dips:** inspect the exact moment. Remove repetition, shorten setup, clarify confusing narration or replace a weak visual.
- **Spikes/rewatches:** identify which demonstration or explanation viewers replayed; consider making that clearer or a follow-up.
- **Returning viewers and meaningful feedback:** use comments/questions to plan the next video, while ignoring spam and abusive messages.
- **Experiment one variable at a time:** title, thumbnail, opening, pacing or visual style. Keep notes; do not assume one video's result proves a universal rule.

Tags are not a growth shortcut. Use a clear title, useful description, accurate category/audience settings, and relevant chapters when appropriate. Never buy views, fake engagement, spam comments, or promise guaranteed placement.

## 8. How JARVIS can help with the current Android controller

The Android Companion currently supports bounded actions:
- Open an installed app by an unambiguous launcher name (for example, Canva).
- Read a bounded summary of visible labels after the user asks to read the screen and grants Accessibility permission.
- Tap a unique, visible, accessible label; scroll; Back/Home; and type bounded text into a field that the user has already focused.
- AI device-action plans need explicit user approval.

It **cannot** currently perform arbitrary coordinate taps, reliable drag-and-drop, unrestricted gestures, fine timeline trimming, exact multi-track audio mixing, full stick-figure rigging, or silently publish/export. Canva may expose some controls differently on mobile, and not every control is accessible to Android's Accessibility service. JARVIS should guide the user step by step, verify what is visible, and stop when a control is not exposed—never pretend an action succeeded.

### Safe Canva assistance flow
1. Ask the user to choose a project type (short or long-form), topic and language.
2. Draft the title options, hook, outline, narration, storyboard and thumbnail concept.
3. Ask before opening Canva; then use the installed-app launcher only when the user explicitly requests it.
4. Guide the user through the visible Canva controls one step at a time. Do not guess hidden UI labels.
5. Let the user perform drag/drop and timing edits that the controller cannot do reliably.
6. Ask the user to preview the export and confirm that the right file is selected.
7. Keep upload/publish as a separate review step; never publish without the account owner's explicit confirmation and required review.

## 9. What the JARVIS Creator Studio workspace now does

The `Creator Studio` dashboard view added in this feature branch is separate from the existing Media Studio renderer:

- Builds a time-coded, 8-chapter long-form outline for a selected 4–20 minute target duration.
- Creates truthful title and opening-hook options, a chapter plan, a YouTube description draft, and a visual direction for each chapter.
- Can send the outline to the existing JARVIS chat endpoint to request a full spoken narration draft. Availability and quality depend on the server's configured AI provider; every factual claim still needs review.
- Exports the narration and plan as Markdown, the chapter/shot plan as CSV, and the description as text.
- Generates a consistent stick-figure pose as an SVG, plus a reusable pose sheet for upload into Canva.
- Provides a small analytics review worksheet that compares user-entered CTR and 30-second retention values with the creator's own channel baseline. It is a manual worksheet, not a YouTube Analytics API connection.
- Adds an optional Creator Coach skill draft flow. JARVIS creates and validates the skill draft first; the user must explicitly approve it before activation.
- Saves the current outline and narration in the current browser/device's local storage. Export a copy to move it to another device.

The Creator Studio planner does **not** render a complete long-form video, generate/record a voice track, or synchronize an editable Canva timeline. The generated SVGs and CSV are handoff assets that the user uploads and arranges in Canva.

## 10. Existing Media Studio and YouTube Publisher limits

The repository's Media Studio supports a storyboard with 2–12 scenes and three formats:
- Vertical short: 720 × 1280, up to 90 seconds.
- Landscape explainer: 1280 × 720, up to 180 seconds.
- Square carousel: 1080 × 1080, up to 60 seconds.

It can draft scenes with heading, voiceover, on-screen text and visual direction; save/approve the storyboard; and export a script, captions, JSON, PNG frames and a silent WebM motion-graphic preview/video. Creator Studio additionally makes a 1280 × 720 PNG thumbnail from editable text, exports a reusable SVG character/pose sheet, and can record a short silent WebM stick-figure sample in browsers that support canvas recording. The current renderers do not record voiceover or create a full Canva timeline edit. It remains a short storyboard/renderer, not an 8–20 minute full video renderer.

The existing YouTube Publisher supports an explicit upload preview and confirmation flow for an approved project and a selected video file. It is not an automatic growth service and does not guarantee reach. Public uploads require the documented review checks; keep the owner in control of the final privacy/audience declarations and upload confirmation.

## 11. Direct Canva Connect handoff in this branch

The Creator Studio now includes an optional Canva Connect flow:

1. Register a Canva Connect integration in the Canva Developer Portal. Use the exact callback URL `https://<your-host>/api/integrations/canva/oauth/callback`.
2. Add `JARVIS_CANVA_OAUTH_CLIENT_ID`, `JARVIS_CANVA_OAUTH_CLIENT_SECRET`, and `JARVIS_CANVA_OAUTH_REDIRECT_URI` to the server environment. JARVIS reuses `JARVIS_TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY` to encrypt the Canva refresh token. Never put the client secret in browser JavaScript or commit it to Git.
3. In Creator Studio, connect Canva and approve the requested `asset:read`, `asset:write`, and `design:content:write` scopes.
4. Generate a 1280 × 720 thumbnail in Creator Studio and choose **Create this thumbnail in Canva**. JARVIS uploads the PNG to the Canva asset library and creates a new 16:9 design.
5. Open the returned Canva edit link to make refinements in Canva. You can disconnect from the same panel; JARVIS calls Canva's token-revocation endpoint before deleting the stored encrypted refresh token.

**Important boundary:** this first Canva action inserts the thumbnail as a single flat PNG image. Text and shapes are not separate editable Canva layers. It does not create a complete video timeline, move clips, trim footage, record a voiceover, edit audio tracks, animate the stick figure inside Canva, or publish to YouTube. Those operations still happen in Canva and remain user-reviewed.

Canva's REST APIs support OAuth, asset uploads, design creation and export. Its Design Editing API exposes only its documented element model, not a general-purpose video timeline editor for JARVIS to control. A successful OAuth connection means the user approved the app; it does not imply every Canva editor feature is automatable.
